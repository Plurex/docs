---
title: "Leverage and liquidation"
description: "How paper perpetuals use collateral and leverage, when an account is liquidated, and what happens to a loss bigger than the account."
---

Paper perpetuals trade with leverage and can be liquidated, so a forward test shows what leverage does to a strategy,
not only how its orders fill. You choose leverage per market, margin is held and locked at it, and an account that
falls to maintenance margin has its positions closed through the live book.

These rules apply on every paper host, and each host takes its values from its model host: which assets count as
collateral, their weights and the leverage each market allows. A live host has its own rules; see
[From paper to live](/paper/going-live#leverage-and-liquidation).

## Collateral

Collateral is a single cross pool shared by every perpetual on the account, modelled on the model host's account. The
whole pool backs every position.

- **The model host decides what counts.** The pool uses the model host's main collateral assets and their weights, so
  both differ between paper hosts. A weight of 1 counts an asset at full value, and a lower weight discounts it. Any
  other asset adds nothing, so deposit one the model host accepts to trade its perpetuals. `totalCollateralValue` in
  [List margin states](/api-reference/platform/hosts/list-margin-states) shows what a deposit added.
- **One valuation asset.** The pool is one margin group, valued in the `valueAsset` that
  [List margin states](/api-reference/platform/hosts/list-margin-states) reports. Each collateral asset is converted at
  a live reference price.
- **Equity** is your free collateral at its weights, plus locked margin, plus all unrealised profit and loss at the mark
  price, gains included. It is reported as `totalCollateralValue`, and as `collateral.total` in
  [Get market state](/api-reference/platform/hosts/get-market-state).

## Leverage

Leverage is a setting on a market for your account. Read it with
[Get leverage](/api-reference/platform/hosts/get-leverage) and change it with
[Set leverage](/api-reference/platform/hosts/set-leverage):

```json
{
  "accountId": "acc_123",
  "instrument": { "type": "Perp", "base": "BTC", "quote": "USDT" },
  "leverage": "10"
}
```

The paper market's `settings.leverage` gives the values it accepts, which are the model host's. See
[Order rules](/capabilities/order-rules#leverage).

- **Default.** A market you have not set uses its `min`, normally 1×.
- **Changing it.** The market must be flat: with a position or an open order there, Set leverage returns
  `422 Rejected`. Setting the value already in force always succeeds. A change reaches that market only.
- **Trigger orders.** A waiting trigger order does not block a change, and uses the leverage in force when it fires.
- **Position mode.** Your leverage carries across a position mode change, and hedging legs both use it.
- **No leverage setting.** A market whose `settings.leverage` is `null` trades at 1×, owes no maintenance margin and is
  never liquidated. Spot markets have no leverage.

## Margin

| When | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| An order is accepted | It holds its margin, its value divided by leverage, plus its fee on the **full** value at the higher of your maker and taker rates. A limit order is valued at its price, and a market order at the average of the book levels it needs, widened by its protection. An order the available collateral cannot cover returns `422 InsufficientFunds`. |
| It fills | The fill locks its initial margin: its value at the fill price, divided by leverage. Locked margin stays at the opening price and does not follow the mark. |
| It ends | What it held and did not use is released. |
| The position closes | Margin is released in proportion to the size closed, and profit, loss and fees post to the settlement asset. |

A closing order needs no margin and is never refused for lack of funds. An order that reverses a netting position needs
margin for its new side; if that cannot be covered, the whole order is rejected with `422 InsufficientFunds`.

**Available collateral**, which is what you can open with, is the weighted collateral not held or locked, less any
negative balances at full value and the pool's net unrealised loss. Gains offset losses across your positions but never
add to what is available, so profit funds new positions only once it is realised. A position opened with everything
available leaves nothing as soon as it starts to lose.

## When liquidation starts

**Maintenance margin** is what an open position must keep: half the initial margin at the market's maximum leverage.

$$
\text{maintenance} = \text{value at mark} \times \frac{1}{2 \times \text{max leverage}}
$$

Use `settings.leverage.max` from the market. On a market with a maximum of 100, maintenance is 0.5% of the position's
value. If the market's maximum changes, so does the maintenance on your open positions.

Liquidation starts when the pool's equity falls to its total maintenance margin. That is when
[List margin states](/api-reference/platform/hosts/list-margin-states) reports a `maintenanceMarginUsage` of 1.

### Your leverage does not move the liquidation price

Maintenance does not depend on the leverage you chose, and the whole pool backs each position, so leverage changes what
stays available for new orders, not where the position is closed. Take 1,000 USDT counted at full value and a long of
0.08 BTC at 50,000, worth 4,000, on a market with a maximum of 100, ignoring fees:

| | At 10× | At 5× |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Margin locked | 400 | 800 |
| Available after opening | 600 | 200 |
| Liquidation price | 37,688.44 | 37,688.44 |

Equity reaches maintenance when 1,000 + 0.08 × (mark − 50,000) = 0.08 × mark × 0.005. What moves the price is more
collateral, a different position size, or profit and loss on your other positions. With collateral at a weight below
1, leverage can move it slightly, because locked margin counts in full and free collateral at its weight.

<Note>
  1× is not exempt. A short at 1× can be liquidated, because its loss has no limit.
</Note>

Spot orders do not check your margin, so spending collateral on an asset the pool does not count lowers your equity
and can bring on a liquidation.

## What happens in a liquidation

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open orders are cancelled">
    Paper cancels your open perpetual orders, waiting trigger orders included. Spot orders stay. These read like any
    other cancelled order: the order has no field for why it was cancelled.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The largest position closes">
    Paper submits a reduce-only market order for the largest position by value, at up to the size the book is showing.
    It fills through the live book like any market order, one fill per price level at your taker rate, and posts the
    usual `Settlement` and `TradingFee` transactions. Any size left over waits for a later order.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Paper checks again">
    One liquidation order runs at a time. When it ends, paper values the account again, and stops once equity is back
    above maintenance. In a hedged market the larger leg closes first and the other can survive.
  </Step>
</Steps>

A liquidation order is an order you did not place. It appears in the [order stream](/guides/streams#account-orders),
order history and fills, with `origin` set to `Liquidation` and `clientOrderId` set to `null`, so check `origin` before
treating an unknown order as a fault. You cannot cancel it: Cancel all skips it and a cancel has no effect. Re-placing
your own closing orders does not hold it off.

Paper does not liquidate on doubtful data. If a price the account depends on is out of date, or a fill is still
settling, it leaves the account alone and looks again, so a position can stay open past its liquidation price for a
while. A market with nothing on the side of the book it would close against is skipped until it has.

## Losses beyond the account

A liquidation closes at real book prices, so in a fast market a position can lose more than the account holds.

- **Written off.** Once the pool has no open positions and is worth less than nothing with its collateral at full value,
  paper writes it off to zero with a positive `Adjustment` transaction, which has no instrument. This applies however
  the position closed, including by your own order. The account's transactions still sum to its wallets.
- **Still owed.** A negative balance your collateral can cover at full value stays owed, for example USDT owed with BTC
  still in the account. Deposit to clear it. Until then it counts against your available collateral at full value,
  though it never stops you closing a position.

## Read it back

| To see | Read |
| --- | --- |
| Where a position would be liquidated | `liquidationPrice` on each side, from [List open positions](/api-reference/platform/hosts/list-positions) or Get market state |
| How close the account is | `maintenanceMarginUsage` and `totalMaintenanceMargin`, from List margin states |
| Margin in use | `totalInitialMargin`: what open orders hold plus what positions lock |
| What you can open | `collateral.available`, from Get market state |
| The leverage in force | `currentLeverageSetting`, from Get market state, or Get leverage |
| What a liquidation did | Orders with `origin` set to `Liquidation`, their [fills](/api-reference/platform/hosts/list-fills) and any `Adjustment` in [List transactions](/api-reference/platform/hosts/list-transactions) |

`liquidationPrice` is an estimate that holds everything else still. Paper reports `null` for a position that shares its
base asset with another open position, hedged legs included, because their marks move together and no single price
answers it. It also reports `null` when no price would liquidate the position. On an account already past maintenance,
the price can sit on the profitable side of the mark until the position closes.
