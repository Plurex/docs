---
title: "Paper trading"
description: "Forward test strategies against live order books with simulated funds, through the same API calls you use to trade for real."
---

Paper trading lets you run a strategy against today's real market without risking money. A paper account trades on a
**paper host**: a simulation of a real host that matches your orders against the venue's live order book and public
trades, charges trading fees, pays and receives perpetual funding, and keeps positions and balances, all with simulated
funds.

Use it as the step between your own backtests and live trading. Your backtests tell you how a strategy behaved on
history; paper trading shows how it behaves in the market as it is now, with live depth, real queues and real funding.
When you are ready, you switch the same code to a connected account. See
[From paper to live](/paper/going-live).

<Note>
  Plurex does not replay history or run backtests. Paper accounts trade forward, in real time, on live data.
</Note>

## What paper simulates

Paper models the parts of a live account that change a strategy's results, not only the fills.

| Area | What paper does |
| --- | --- |
| [Matching](#how-orders-fill) | Marketable orders walk the live visible book with real slippage. Resting orders queue behind the visible size at their price and fill as live trades work through the queue. |
| [Trigger orders](#trigger-orders) | Stops and take-profits watch the live last trade, best bid or ask, mark or index price. |
| [Wallets and collateral](#positions-collateral-and-balances) | Balances are held and locked as on the host. Perpetual collateral is a weighted, multi-asset pool modelled on the host's account. |
| [Positions](#positions-collateral-and-balances) | Spot, linear and inverse perpetuals, in netting or hedging mode, with realised and unrealised profit and loss. |
| [Fees](/paper/fees-and-funding#trading-fees) | Maker and taker rates from fee tiers modelled on the exchange's schedule, including rebates. |
| [Funding](/paper/fees-and-funding#perpetual-funding) | Perpetuals pay and receive funding at the venue's real funding times, from its finalised rates. |
| [The API](#the-same-api-as-a-real-account) | Every host operation and both account streams, the same as a real account, with leverage the one exception. |

## Paper hosts

A paper host is a host like any other. You find it with [List hosts](/api-reference/platform/hosts/list), and these
fields mark it out:

| Field | On a paper host |
| --- | --- |
| `isPaper` | `true` |
| `modelHostId` | The ID of the real host it simulates, its **model host** |
| `venueIds` | Exactly one venue: the venue whose live data it trades against |
| `supportedCredentialTypes` | `["Paper"]`: no exchange credentials needed |

Paper markets take their order rules from the model host: the same instruments, price and size increments, minimums,
value floors, and the same order types and order options where paper can simulate them. Paper simulates spot, linear
perpetual and inverse perpetual markets, so its market list can be smaller than the model host's. Always read the
paper host's own markets with [List host markets](/api-reference/platform/hosts/list-markets) rather than assuming
they match.

A paper host publishes its own [capabilities](/capabilities/host-capabilities). It supports every order query and
cancel variant, fill lookup and account stream whatever its model host offers, so in places it offers more than the
model host (for example `clientOrderId` lookups, time in force options or fill details on the order stream). Its one
gap is leverage control. Read the paper host's capabilities while you paper trade, and the model
host's before you go live.

<Tip>
  Host IDs such as `bybit-paper` and `valr-paper` appear in examples, but discover the paper hosts available to you
  with [List hosts](/api-reference/platform/hosts/list) instead of hard-coding them.
</Tip>

### The same API as a real account

Once created, a paper account is an ordinary account. It uses:

- every `/hosts` operation except leverage: orders, fills, wallets, positions, margin states, market state, position
  mode and transactions;
- both account streams, [order changes](/api-reference/platform/hosts/subscribe-to-account-orders) and
  [transactions](/api-reference/platform/hosts/subscribe-to-account-transactions);
- the account operations [List](/api-reference/platform/accounts/list), [Get](/api-reference/platform/accounts/get),
  [Rename](/api-reference/platform/accounts/rename) and [Delete](/api-reference/platform/accounts/delete).

The only operations specific to paper are [Create a paper account](/api-reference/platform/paper/create),
[Deposit](/api-reference/platform/paper/deposit), [Withdraw](/api-reference/platform/paper/withdraw) and the three
[fee tier operations](/paper/fees-and-funding).

## Create and fund an account

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create the account">
    Call [Create a paper account](/api-reference/platform/paper/create) with your tenant ID, a name and the **paper
    host's** ID (not the model host's).

    ```json
    {
      "tenantId": "ten_abc",
      "name": "Momentum forward test",
      "hostId": "bybit-paper"
    }
    ```

    The response is the new account, with `isPaper: true`. It starts with no balances and with the default fee tier;
    see [Fees and funding](/paper/fees-and-funding) to choose another.

    Creating an account is not safe to repeat: a repeat creates a second account. If the outcome is unclear, check
    [List accounts](/api-reference/platform/accounts/list) before retrying. If your plan's paper-account limit is
    reached, the call returns `422 AccountLimit`.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Deposit simulated funds">
    Call [Deposit](/api-reference/platform/paper/deposit) with the account ID, a positive amount and the asset. The
    response is the resulting wallet.

    ```json
    {
      "accountId": "acc_123",
      "amount": "10000",
      "asset": "USDT"
    }
    ```

    Each deposit posts a `Deposit` transaction. Deposits are not safe to repeat. If the outcome is unclear, check
    [List transactions](/api-reference/platform/hosts/list-transactions) where the paper host's
    `accountTransactions.listTransactions` is supported, or compare the balance from
    [List wallets](/api-reference/platform/hosts/list-wallets), before retrying.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Deposit the asset your orders will spend or post as collateral:

| You want to | Deposit |
| --- | --- |
| Buy on a spot market | The quote asset, for example USDT for BTC/USDT |
| Sell on a spot market | The base asset, for example BTC for BTC/USDT |
| Trade a linear perpetual | A collateral asset, typically the quote or settlement asset, for example USDT |
| Trade an inverse perpetual | The base asset, for example BTC for an inverse BTC/USD perpetual, which settles and charges fees in base |

### Withdraw to set up a scenario

[Withdraw](/api-reference/platform/paper/withdraw) debits a paper account and returns the resulting wallet. Nothing
leaves Plurex; use it to test how your strategy behaves with a low or empty balance, or with a wallet too small for its
next order. The request has the same shape as a deposit.

- Only the available balance can be withdrawn. Funds held by open orders or locked as position margin cannot, so
  cancel orders or close positions first.
- A withdrawal above the available balance, including from an asset the account does not hold, fails with
  `422 InsufficientFunds` and changes nothing.
- Each withdrawal posts a `Withdrawal` transaction with a negative amount.
- Withdrawals are not safe to repeat: a repeat withdraws again. If the outcome is unclear, check List transactions
  before retrying.

There is no reset. To start again from a clean slate, create a new paper account. You can also deposit and create paper
accounts in the [web app](/platform/overview).

## How orders fill

Paper fills your orders against what the venue is actually showing. Every paper account is matched on its own: paper
orders never use up each other's liquidity, and no paper order touches the real market. Each account sees what its
orders would have done if it were the only one trading alongside the real market.

```mermaid
flowchart TD
    A[SubmitOrder] --> P{Post-only and crosses?}
    P -- Yes --> X[Cancelled, nothing filled]
    P -- No --> B{Crosses the live book?}
    B -- "Yes: market order or marketable limit" --> C[Walks visible depth level by level<br/>One fill at the average price, as taker]
    C --> D{Size left over?}
    D -- "GTC limit" --> E[Remainder rests at its limit price]
    D -- "IOC" --> F[Remainder cancelled]
    D -- "FOK and not fully fillable" --> G[Whole order cancelled, nothing filled]
    B -- "No, IOC or FOK" --> X
    B -- "No, GTC limit" --> E
    E --> H[Joins the queue behind the visible size at its price]
    H --> I[Public trades at its price work through the queue ahead,<br/>then fill the order]
    H --> J[Trades or book moving through its price fill it]
    I --> K[Fills at its own price, as maker]
    J --> K
```

### Taking liquidity

A market order, or a limit order that crosses the book when it arrives, takes liquidity immediately. It walks the
opposite side of the live book level by level, as far as the visible depth allows, and is reported as one fill at the
volume-weighted average price of the levels it took. Large orders therefore see realistic slippage across the visible
depth.

- **IOC**: whatever the visible depth cannot fill is cancelled. A partly filled order ends `Cancelled` with
  `sizeTraded` above zero.
- **FOK**: all or nothing. If visible depth cannot fill the whole size (at or better than the limit price, for a
  limit order), the order is cancelled with nothing filled.
- **GTC limit**: the remainder rests on the book at the limit price.

### Resting orders

When a limit order rests, it joins the back of the queue: paper notes the size already visible at its price on its
side of the book. Your place in the queue then moves as the market does:

- Public trades at your price that hit your side of the book work through the queue ahead; once it has traded, further
  trades fill your order.
- When the visible size at your price shrinks, you move forward with it.
- A trade at a better price than yours, or opposite liquidity in the book at or through your price, fills the order
  directly.
- Your own resting orders share liquidity in price and time priority: best price first, then oldest.

A resting order always fills at its own limit price, as maker, and is charged the maker rate. It can fill over many
trades; each fill arrives on the order stream with its details.

### Time in force and order options

| Behaviour | What paper does |
| --- | --- |
| Limit time in force | GTC, IOC and FOK |
| Market time in force | IOC (the default) and FOK |
| Post-only order that would cross on arrival | Cancelled, not filled |
| IOC limit order that does not cross | Cancelled immediately |
| FOK limit order that does not cross | Cancelled immediately |
| Reduce-only order larger than the position left to close, in netting mode | Rejected with `422 Rejected`, not trimmed. Other open orders that would close the position count against what is left. |
| `Close` order larger than the leg, in hedging mode | Trimmed to what is left of the leg |
| Reduce-only order with no position, or on the wrong side | Rejected |
| Order that partly fills and keeps resting | `LIVE`, with `sizeTraded` above zero |

Which options each market accepts is in its order rules. Read them from
[List host markets](/api-reference/platform/hosts/list-markets) and see [Order rules](/capabilities/order-rules).

### What is not modelled

- **Configurable latency.** Paper adds no artificial delay, and you cannot set one. Your orders still see real
  latency: they travel through Plurex and are matched against live market data as it arrives, just as a live order
  travels to the exchange.
- **Market impact beyond the visible book.** Paper does not deplete the book after your order takes from it, so
  orders in quick succession each see the same visible liquidity until the venue's book updates.
- **Hidden liquidity and venue-specific queue rules.** Queue position is estimated from visible size and public
  trades.
- **Fills per level.** A sweep across several levels is reported as one fill at the average price, where a live venue
  may report several.
- **Venue limits beyond the order rules.** Paper applies the market's published order rules, not an exchange's price
  bands or maximum order sizes, and has no market-order price protection: a market order can take the whole visible
  book.
- **Self-trade prevention.** Your own orders never trade with each other.

## Trigger orders

Paper supports trigger limit and trigger market orders on markets where the model host offers them. A trigger watches
one reference price (last trade, best bid, best ask, mark or index) and fires when that price reaches or passes your
trigger price in the direction of the condition, `RefPriceGTE` or `RefPriceLTE`.

- Paper offers only the reference prices it can evaluate from the venue's live data, and evaluates exactly the kind
  the order uses. Read the `trigger` section of the paper market's `triggerLimit` and `triggerMarket` order rules to
  see which kinds are offered and whether you choose one (`UserSelectable`) or the host fixes it (`HostSelected`).
- Every live value is checked in order, so a brief cross is not missed. A trigger whose condition is already met when
  you place it fires immediately.
- A fired trigger market order fills against the book like a market order. A fired trigger limit order crosses or rests
  like a limit order.
- **Funds are held from submission.** A waiting trigger order reserves the funds or collateral it needs, so it counts
  against your available balance before it fires.

Reduce-only trigger orders on perpetuals make stop-loss and take-profit orders.

## Positions, collateral and balances

### Wallets

Each asset has one wallet with a total and an available balance. Open orders hold funds and open positions lock them,
as on the host; what is held or locked is not available to new orders or withdrawals. You can deposit any asset.

**Spot.** A buy holds quote and a sell holds base while the order is open; a cancel releases the hold. Fills move the
balances. An order the balance cannot cover returns `422 InsufficientFunds`. Spot trades do not post `Settlement`
transactions; their fees post as `TradingFee`.

### Collateral on perpetuals

Paper trades perpetuals at 1×, without leverage: opening risk needs collateral equal to the position's value. An order
that needs more collateral than is available is rejected with `422 Rejected`.

Collateral is a single cross pool shared by every perpetual on the account, modelled on the model host's account:

- **Several assets count.** The pool uses the model host's main collateral assets. Stablecoins count at full value;
  assets such as BTC and ETH count at a discount (a weight below 1) to their value.
- **One valuation asset.** The pool is valued in one asset, reported as `baseCollateralAsset` in
  [List margin states](/api-reference/platform/hosts/list-margin-states). Each collateral asset is converted at a live
  reference price.
- **What you can open** is the weighted available collateral, less what open orders hold and positions lock, less any
  negative balances at full value. Unrealised profit does not add to it.
- **Reading it.** List margin states and [Get market state](/api-reference/platform/hosts/get-market-state) report
  the pool's total and available collateral. The total includes unrealised profit and loss at the mark price.

Assets outside the pool add nothing as collateral. Deposit one of the model host's collateral assets, typically its
stablecoins, to trade its perpetuals.

### Positions and profit and loss

- Linear and inverse perpetuals each use their own contract maths: see [Contract details](/capabilities/order-rules#contract-details-what-a-size-means).
- Realised profit and loss posts to the settlement asset as a `Settlement` transaction on every fill that reduces or
  closes a position.
- There is no liquidation. Losses, fees and funding can take a balance negative. A negative balance blocks new risk.
- A closing order is never refused for lack of funds. An order that reverses a netting position needs collateral for
  its new side; if that cannot be covered, the whole order is rejected.

### Position mode

New perpetual positions start in netting mode. Where a market offers hedging, switch with
[Set position mode](/api-reference/platform/hosts/set-position-mode) while the market is flat and has no open orders.
In netting mode, an order larger than the position closes it and opens the other way. In hedging mode, long and short
legs are independent, each with its own entry price, profit and loss, fees and funding, and each fully backed by
collateral.

In hedging mode every order must set `positionEffect`: `Open` adds to the leg on the order's side, and `Close` reduces
the opposite leg. Paper rejects an order without it with `422 Rejected`.

### Leverage

[Get leverage](/api-reference/platform/hosts/get-leverage) and [Set leverage](/api-reference/platform/hosts/set-leverage)
are not supported on paper hosts, every paper market's `settings.leverage` is `null`, and market state reports the
leverage setting as `Disabled`.

## What paper does not simulate

| Not simulated | What happens instead |
| --- | --- |
| Leverage | Perpetuals trade at 1× collateral |
| Liquidation, maintenance margin, auto-deleveraging | Balances can go negative; a closing order is never refused for lack of funds |
| Borrowing, interest and isolated margin | Not available |
| Dated futures and options | Not listed on paper hosts |
| Resets | Create a new account to start fresh; use Withdraw to lower a balance |
| Volume-based fee tiers, promotions, fee-token discounts | You select a fee tier manually |
| Configurable latency and market impact | Orders see the real latency of Plurex and live market data, against the visible book and trades |
